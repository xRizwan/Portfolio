**The short version:** the handwritten gradients were correct. Several ReLU units stopped responding during training, and with only four hidden units there was too little capacity left to learn XOR.

<h2 id="problem">Four inputs. Inconsistent results.</h2>

My first version had four hidden units with ReLU. Some runs solved XOR; others stopped around 75% accuracy. I initially suspected a mistake in the handwritten gradients.

The problem was dead ReLUs. Some units produced zero for every input. Their gradients were also zero, so they could not recover. With only four hidden units, losing several of them left too little useful capacity.

<Callout>
  <strong>What changed:</strong> a wider hidden layer and LeakyReLU made learning more consistent in
  this experiment.
</Callout>

He initialization did not fix the same failing seeds. The practical change was giving the network more working units and preserving a nonzero gradient on the negative side.

<pre class="code">
  <code>{`model = Sequential()
model.add(Dense(8, input_dim=2))
model.add(LeakyReLU())
model.add(Dense(1, input_dim=8))
model.add(Sigmoid())`}</code>
</pre>

<h2 id="lesson">A small but important limit</h2>

All four possible XOR inputs are in the training data. This demonstrates whether the network can fit the function. It does not demonstrate generalization to new data.

Writing the layers myself made this failure easier to inspect. I could follow the values and gradients instead of treating training as a single call to a library.
